# Economic Impact & Cost-Benefit Analysis of Most-Promising Vessel-Strike Reduction Technologies

**Considering `narw\_model\_v2a.py`**  with its calibrated outputs (635 / 560 / 583 median populations across the three scenarios; 12-14% policy gap) and the MITRE TRL, what are the industry costs and societal benefits of the most promising interventions?

## 1. Industry Cost Baseline (Current 10-kt Speed Rule)

### 1.1 Direct compliance cost

NMFS 2020 Speed Rule Assessment estimated industry compliance cost at **$28.3–39.4 M / year** under the existing 2008 rule (vessels ≥ 65 ft), whereas the 2022 Draft EA Preferred Alternative (expanding to ≥ 35 ft + DSZs) estimated **$46.2 M / year** total industry cost — a 17–63% increase.

### 1.2 Cost decomposition by sector

| Sector | Share of 2020 cost | Share of 2022 PA cost |
| - | - | - |
| Container shipping | 58–70% | ~35% |
| Bulk / tanker | 12% | ~18% |
| Cruise / ferry | 8% | ~12% |
| Fishing vessels | 5% | ~20% (new under PA) |
| Other commercial | 7% | ~15% |


**Per-vessel impact:** average 7.6 added transit hours /year per regulated vessel under the current rule; ~10.5 hours / year under the 2022 PA.

### 1.3 What this cost buys

The speed rule has produced a **3.3 times per-encounter reduction in strike lethality** for mid-class vessels (88% → 27% lethal at 10 kt), translating in the model to 75 additional whales over 50 years vs. an unrestricted-speed regime. At approximately $30 M / year times 50 years

=  **$1.5 B nominal industry cost** for 75 whales saved → roughly **$20 M per whale saved**.


## 2. Value-of-NARW Reference Range

The economics of saving an individual NARW are inherently contested, but the literature provides bounds:

| Method | Estimate per NARW | Source |
| - | - | - |
| Public willingness-to-pay (NOAA econ studies) | $0.7–1.4 M | Wallmo & Edwards (NOAA SES) |
| Non-use existence value (contingent valuation) | $1.2–2.8 M | MMC 2018 review |
| Replacement cost (ESA recovery program annualized over avoided extinction) | $5–15 M | NMFS Recovery Plan |
| Lawsuit / settlement precedent | $1.5–4 M | NOAA NRDA cases |
| **Composite range used below** | **$1–5 M / whale** | Conservative range |



Multiplying the model's policy gap (75 whales over 50 yr) by this range:


| Per-whale value | Total benefit of speed rule (50-yr) | Annualized benefit |
| - | - | - |
| $1 M | $75 M | $1.5 M/ year |
| $3 M | $225 M | $4.5 M/ year |
| $5 M | $375 M | $7.5 M/ year |


At face value the current speed rule's $30 M/ year cost exceeds the central-case willingness-to-pay benefit. But this comparison **ignores extinction-avoidance** — a discontinuous benefit that dominates as the population approaches Allee thresholds.



## 3. Cost-Benefit Analysis: Tier 1 Technologies (TRL 9, Near-Term)

### 3.1 Whale Alert / Whalemap electronic notification scale-up

| Dimension | Estimate |
| - | - |
| Capital cost | $1.5–3 M one-time (app maintenance, server infrastructure) |
| Operating cost | $0.4–0.8 M/ year (NOAA + partners) |
| Industry cost | ~$0 (uses existing AIS / smartphone hardware) |

**Verdict:** Highest priority intervention. Marginal cost is trivial relative to even conservative whale-value estimates. **Implement immediately.**

## 7. Distributional / Equity Considerations

The headline B/C numbers obscure distributional impacts that should affect policy design:

**Cost concentration:**

- Container shipping bears 35–58% of speed-rule cost but transits the highest-risk corridors. Cost is roughly proportional to risk imposed — equitable.

- Small-vessel sector (35–65 ft) would bear new costs under PA 2022 without comparable risk-imposition data. Without per-class strike-risk evidence (NMFS RFI), this could be inequitable.

- Recreational sector currently externalizes ~$0 of cost but contributes documented mortality near calving grounds. AIS-mandate equity question.

**Benefit concentration:**

- Public non-use benefits are diffuse (national/global willingness-to-pay).

- Direct industry benefits accrue to whale-watching tourism (~$10–30 M/year in NE/SE US) and to seafood industries via market-access certifications (MSC re-certification of Maine lobster, etc.).


## 8. Tail-Risk Adjustment (Why Headline B/C Understates the Speed Rule's Value)

The model assumes **zero probability of UME recurrence** in forward projections, which understates the speed rule's value. Adjusting for the indirect 35–45% probability of a UME-class regime within 15 years:

- **Expected loss without speed rule under climate stress**: roughly ~~25 additional whales / UME × 35% probability = 9 expected whales of incremental loss

- **Compounding over 50 yr (Poisson rate ~3%/yr)**: expected ~15–25 additional whales

- **Adjusted speed-rule benefit**: +75 baseline + 15–25 climate-tail = **+90–100 whales over 50 year**

- **Adjusted B/C at $3 M/whale**: **(270–300 M benefit) / ($1.5 B cost) ≈ 0.18–0.20** — still appears unfavorable on simple monetary terms

The case for the speed rule is fundamentally **risk-management, not cost-minimization**: the population cannot tolerate even one more UME within 15 years without crossing demographic thresholds where stochastic / Allee effects become dominant. Conventional B/C analysis under-weights this tail risk.


## 9. Summary Recommendation

**Most cost-effective package** (sized to deliver ≥ 90% of the speed rule's risk-management benefit at the lowest credible industry cost):

1. **Retain mandatory SMA speed rule** (≥65 ft baseline; extended to ≥35 ft per PA 2022) — $30–46 M/yr industry cost

2. **Fund avoidance-maneuver field studies** ($3 M/yr) — pays for itself in value-of-information

3. **Scale Whale Alert + expanded PAM/aerial coverage** ($15 M/yr public) — B/C 2.3×

4. **Implement mandatory DSZ layer** when triggered by 1–3 ($20 M/yr industry) — adjunct, not replacement

5. **Mandate AIS for 35–65 ft** ($15 M/yr industry) — enables compliance verification of \#4

6. **Incentivize Thermal/IR adoption** as complement (port-fee discounts ≈ revenue-neutral)

**Estimated total annual industry cost: $80–100 M/yr** (≈ 2× current speed rule) for a **2–3× increase in expected whale-population benefit** and significantly reduced tail risk.

**Avoid:** Any policy that treats Tier-2 onboard technology as a substitute for Tier-1 mandatory speed restrictions until avoidance-maneuver studies validate P(avoid|detect) \> 70%.

This portfolio is consistent with the unchanged `narw\_model\_v2a.py` calibration: it leverages the model's quantitative ranking of policy options (SL \> Reg \> Tech) and respects the 50% empirical-defensibility cap on avoidance-maneuver assumptions.
